# Immigration's Impact on the U.S.: What the Research Shows

Oct 24, 2026 · @Zach Jusice

Most expert disagreement on immigration is about how much, how fast, and who gets admitted, not whether it helps or hurts overall. This is a review of the most-cited studies from across the political spectrum, not a formal statistical meta-analysis. Each source's lean is noted. No study models truly "unrestricted" immigration; the 2021–2024 surge is the closest real-world test, and several studies below examine it directly.

## Context

Southwest border encounters hit a record 2,475,669 in fiscal year 2023, up 4% from FY2022 ([House Homeland Security Committee](https://homeland.house.gov/2023/10/21/chairman-green-on-historic-september-border-numbers-mayorkas-policies-have-made-us-less-safe-secretary-must-be-held-accountable/)). The flow has since reversed: Brookings (center-left) estimates net migration was negative in 2025, between −295,000 and −10,000, the first time in at least 50 years ([ABC News](https://abc7news.com/post/us-1st-time-50-years-experienced-negative-net-migration-2025-report/18398145/)).

## Economic growth

The research consensus is that immigration raises long-run economic growth, with short-term pressure on wages, interest rates and housing.

- **[National Academies of Sciences, 2016](https://www.nationalacademies.org/news/new-report-assesses-the-economic-and-fiscal-consequences-of-immigration)** (nonpartisan, 22-expert panel): the closest thing to a consensus review. Found an overall positive impact on long-run U.S. economic growth.
- **[CBO, 2024](https://cbo.gov/publication/60165)** (nonpartisan): projected the 2021–2026 surge adds $1.2 trillion in federal revenue over 2024–2034, against $0.3 trillion in added mandatory spending and interest. CBO also expected it to initially slow wage growth and push up interest rates and prices, especially housing.

## Taxes and public budgets

This is the real tradeoff: the federal budget gains, state and local budgets lose, and the result depends heavily on immigrants' age and education.

| Source | Lean | Finding |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [CBO, 2024](https://cbo.gov/publication/60165) | Nonpartisan | Surge cuts federal deficits by a net $0.9 trillion over 2024–2034 |
| [CBO, 2025](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61464) | Nonpartisan | Surge cost state and local governments a direct net $9.2 billion in 2023 (0.3% of their spending), mainly K–12 schools, shelter and border security |
| [Manhattan Institute, 2024](https://manhattan.institute/article/the-lifetime-fiscal-impact-of-immigrants) | Center-right | Average new immigrant cuts deficits by $10,000+ over a lifetime; those without a college degree, or arriving after 55, cost up to $400,000 each; recent border surge costs about $1.15 trillion over immigrants' lifetimes |
| [Cato Institute](https://cato.org/working-paper/manhattan-institutes-lifetime-fiscal-impact-immigrants-report-shows-upside) | Libertarian, pro-immigration | Reworking the Manhattan model with different assumptions makes the recent unlawful entrants a net positive of about $4.9 trillion |
| [Center for Immigration Studies](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/?p=1220456) | Restrictionist, right | Net fiscal drain of $65,292 per illegal immigrant, excluding children |

## Wages

The overall wage effect on native workers is small; any harm falls mainly on the lowest-skilled, and economists still disagree on its size.

- **[National Academies](https://www.nationalacademies.org/publications/23550)** (nonpartisan): long-term effect on native wages and employment overall is very small; negative effects most likely for earlier immigrants and native-born high school dropouts.
- **[George Borjas](https://newrepublic.com/article/76864/number-the-day)** (Harvard; skeptic, often cited by restrictionists): found 1980–2000 immigration cut average native wages 3.2%, and 8.9% for high school dropouts.
- **[David Card](https://newrepublic.com/article/76864/number-the-day)** (Berkeley; Nobel laureate): found immigrants did not lower natives' wages, even for low-skilled workers.

## Housing

A Dallas Fed working paper (nonpartisan, preliminary draft) found each 1% increase in unauthorized immigrant workers raised local home prices about 2.2% and rents about 1.4%, without new construction keeping pace ([Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/politics/biden-era-illegal-immigration-drove-up-housing-costs-fed-economists-find)). The same study found the influx boosted local employment with little measurable effect on wages. The authors note immigration was not the sole driver of rising housing costs.

## Crime

The data consistently show immigrants, including those here illegally, commit crimes at lower rates than native-born citizens.

- **[Texas arrest data, 2012–2018](https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/comparing-crime-rates-between-undocumented-immigrants-legal)** (peer-reviewed, PNAS): compared with undocumented immigrants, U.S.-born citizens were over 2 times as likely to be arrested for violent crimes and over 4 times as likely for property crimes.
- **[Cato Institute](https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/fact-check-team-do-illegal-migrants-commit-more-crime-than-american-citizens-dhs)** (libertarian), 2023 incarceration rates per 100,000: native-born 1,221; illegal immigrants 613; legal immigrants 319.
- **[CBO, 2025](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61464)** (nonpartisan): its literature review found immigrants consistently less likely than native-born citizens to be arrested, convicted or incarcerated.

Caveat: most states don't record immigration status, so the evidence leans heavily on Texas data.

## Bottom line

**Where concerns about uncontrolled immigration have support:**

- Rapid, unscreened surges impose real costs on state and local budgets.
- They put upward pressure on rents and home prices.
- Low-skilled arrivals are likely a lifetime fiscal cost; some pro-immigration analysts accept this but dispute its size.
- Low-wage workers may see some wage pressure, though economists disagree on how much.

**Where they don't:**

- The crime data consistently cut against the fear.
- Overall, immigration is associated with economic growth and federal deficit reduction.
- Young and skilled immigrants are strongly net positive for public finances.
