1“Biden oversaw unusually high unauthorized immigration.”

Against Immigration

The Evidence-Based Case for Stricter U.S. Immigration Controls

Southwest-border encounters reached roughly 2.5 million in fiscal 2023, and the system couldn’t keep up. Immigration courts had a backlog of nearly 3.5 million cases and didn’t track whether people showed up to hearings. A federal audit found more than 31,000 unusable release addresses for unaccompanied children. That doesn’t mean those children were missing, but it shows how thin the tracking was.

  • ICE Cannot Effectively Monitor the Location and Status of All Unaccompanied Alien Children After Federal Custody ↗ DHS Office of Inspector General, Mar. 2025 · Non-partisan

For Immigration

Immigration Under Obama and Biden: What the Evidence Shows

Agrees the surge was real. Biden’s early years saw a much larger arrival surge than Obama’s, and the brief calls the border pressures and local costs “real.” Its point is that a surge in arrivals is a separate question from whether immigration damaged the country overall.

Objective Fact-Check

Did Immigration Under Obama and Biden Harm the United States?

Fact-check verdict Supported.

Border encounters and the estimated unauthorized population reached record levels. Pew estimates the unauthorized population hit about 14 million in 2023. That figure includes some people with temporary permission to stay, and it isn’t the same as the number of border crossings.

Objective Fact-Check

Did Immigration Under Obama and Biden Harm the United States?

Fact-check verdict Misleading.

Obama’s administration carried out substantial formal removals, prioritizing recent border crossers and people convicted of serious crimes. It also limited enforcement against some long-settled immigrants, but calling the record “permissive” doesn’t match it.

3“Immigration burdens taxpayers and public services.”

Against Immigration

The Evidence-Based Case for Stricter U.S. Immigration Controls

CBO estimated above-trend immigration brought state and local governments $10.1 billion in taxes but $19.3 billion in direct costs in 2023, a $9.2 billion net cost (about 0.3% of their spending). New York City spent $1.41 billion on asylum-seeker shelter and services in fiscal 2023 and $3.70 billion in fiscal 2024.

For Immigration

Immigration Under Obama and Biden: What the Evidence Shows

CBO projected the 2021–2026 surge would add $8.9 trillion to GDP over 2024–2034 and cut federal deficits by about $900 billion, because added taxes outweigh added federal spending. Those are projections, not measured results. The brief also accepts CBO’s $9.2 billion state and local cost: both can be true at once.

Objective Fact-Check

Did Immigration Under Obama and Biden Harm the United States?

Fact-check verdict Partly supported.

The recent surge imposed measurable state and local costs, while the projected federal effect is positive. The National Academies found first-generation immigrants cost state and local governments, while their U.S.-born children are among the strongest taxpayers. The Center for Immigration Studies argues unauthorized immigrants are a net fiscal burden; that result depends on accounting choices and time horizon.

4“Immigrants take jobs and reduce Americans’ wages.”

Against Immigration

The Evidence-Based Case for Stricter U.S. Immigration Controls

CBO projected the surge would slow wage growth through 2026 for U.S. workers with 12 or fewer years of education, because of more competition for similar jobs, and would push up housing demand. CBO expects the wage effect to reverse later. This is a short-term, uneven cost, not a nationwide collapse.

Sources (1)
  • Effects of the Immigration Surge on the Federal Budget and the Economy ↗ Congressional Budget Office, July 2024 · Non-partisan

From the full write-up: The Evidence-Based Case for Stricter U.S. Immigration Controls

For Immigration

Immigration Under Obama and Biden: What the Evidence Shows

The National Academies’ broad research review found very small long-term effects on native-born workers’ overall wages and employment. Immigrants add workers and customers. The review did find plausible costs for less-educated workers.

Sources (1)
  • The Economic and Fiscal Consequences of Immigration ↗ National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine, 2017 · Non-partisan

From the full write-up: Immigration Under Obama and Biden: What the Evidence Shows

Objective Fact-Check

Did Immigration Under Obama and Biden Harm the United States?

Fact-check verdict Mostly unsupported as a general claim.

Some lower-wage workers face competition, but broad effects are small or mixed. A Congressional Research Service review of seven studies found past enforcement programs were linked to some wage gains in low-wage markets, mixed results for native-born employment, and job losses in some higher-wage sectors.

6“Stricter border enforcement reduces unauthorized crossings.”

Against Immigration

The Evidence-Based Case for Stricter U.S. Immigration Controls

After Biden’s stricter June 2024 asylum rules, southwest-border encounters fell from about 2.5 million in fiscal 2023 to about 2.1 million in fiscal 2024. That fits the idea that tighter rules reduce border pressure, though encounters aren’t unique people and no single policy explains the drop.

Fact-check verdict Supported.

Border Patrol encounters fell to 237,538 in fiscal 2025, the lowest since 1970, down from more than 2 million in fiscal 2023. Tightening began under Biden in June 2024, and Mexican enforcement and other factors also contributed.

Where all three agree

Read side by side, the three write-ups share more ground than you might expect:

  • The Biden-era surge was historically large.
  • Local governments and cities paid real costs for schools, shelter, and services.
  • CBO projects the federal budget and the overall economy come out ahead.
  • Obama’s record was not the same as Biden’s.
  • Tighter border rules reduced crossings, and the tightening started under Biden in June 2024.
  • Some lower-wage workers feel short-term wage pressure, but broad effects are small.
  • None of the three argues that immigrants commit more crime, or that immigration is inherently harmful.
